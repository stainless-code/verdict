AI review works. It is also metered, gated behind app installs, and hard to audit.
Verdict keeps the review and drops the meter.

## Hosted review trades the bottleneck for a meter

Five pains sit behind Verdict: latency, held keys, unverifiable output, anchor rot, and approval theater.

1. **Latency.** PRs wait days for a human look, or merge unreviewed.
2. **Held keys and meters.** Hosted reviewers want an app install, webhooks, or vaulted keys, and most meter usage — some bill each re-review anew.
3. **Unverifiable output.** Prose with no evidence level, anchor check, or record of what was claimed — authors skim past it.
4. **Anchor rot and re-report spam.** Comments land on moved lines, findings return every push, threads resolve without a fix.
5. **Approval theater.** An approve on an unexamined diff says nothing about what was checked — and since 2026-10-28 a Copilot approval can satisfy a required-approval rule.

## One binary on your machine does fetch, review, gate, post

`fetch` pulls the bundle — on GitHub with no clone — any model you run does the review, and `post` lands one native review.

- **Trust.** Keys stay on your machine; diffs go only to your chosen model — a local one keeps them on the machine. Open Go, no Go dependencies.
- **Proof.** Every finding carries an evidence rung from `said` to `reproduced`; unproven ships labeled or is dropped. Anchors check against hunks; suggestions are real suggestion blocks or the post refuses.
- **Memory.** Signatures without line numbers, a status, and append-only history: re-reviews never re-report, threads retire by rule.
- **Verdicts.** A severity ladder maps to the author's next action; binding verdicts need `--bind`, approval is refused with findings open, and `calibrate` scores predictions against outcomes.

## What Verdict refuses to be

No hosted merge gate, daemon, model provider, or dashboard. `gate` folds open findings, stale HEAD, and open threads into one local exit code honored like any other CI step. Verdict never posts a check run or merges for you.

Enterprise merge gating, fleet policy, and hosted audit trails need a server, so they stay with hosted reviewers.

## Local review is no longer the wedge; the bundle is

CodeRabbit, Greptile, Cursor, Copilot, and Macroscope all review local changes now, each signed in and metered. Verdict's claim is narrower, and checkable: the review target is a plain bundle on disk. `fetch` writes `.verdict/state/<pr>/` with `PR.json`, and `fetch-local` writes
`.verdict/state/local-<name>/` with `source.json`; both carry `full.diff`, `toc.txt` and
`roles.txt`, and nothing stays running. The same files feed `report --check` in CI and `post` on GitHub, GitLab, or Forgejo.

## The long tail meters the same way

Qodo meters credits (`$0.012` each; Pro Team `$30`/mo buys
roughly 18 reviews and packs scale to 20,000 credits, roughly 144) and also publishes
`PR-Agent`, an MIT reviewer with 14k stars; Sourcery sells seats (`$12–$24` per dev/mo
annual) and puts self-hosting, SSO and bring-your-own keys on Enterprise; Ellipsis runs
the harness for you at tokens plus 10%. The closest open-source project is proval, AGPL-3.0, self-hosted, bring your own
model including a local one, for GitLab, Forgejo and GitHub, but it answers "local"
with a Docker Compose service, a dashboard and an encryption key, where Verdict answers
with one binary and no service.

## Complements, not competitors

clawpatch (machine-checked fix-to-revalidate loops for whole-repo campaigns) and pstack
(verification culture and multi-model endurance) shaped this skill's discipline. They
solve different problems and can run after Verdict posts.

## The prompt is shared on purpose; the meter is the disagreement

Verdict's review core descends from Pullfrog's open-source action — the review prompt,
not its harness wrapping, credited in
[NOTICE](https://github.com/stainless-code/verdict/blob/main/NOTICE.md). The craft is shared on purpose; the disagreement is the meter.
