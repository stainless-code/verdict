The real competition is anything that reviews a pull request: CodeRabbit, Greptile,
and Pullfrog, plus a long tail of same-shaped SaaS reviewers (Sourcery, Qodo, cubic:
subscription plus dashboard, different logos) and the status quo of human-only review.

| Reviewer                  | Price and ask                                                                        | Where they win                                                     | What Verdict does differently                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CodeRabbit                | Per-dev subscription, GitHub App plus webhooks                                       | Polish, merge gating, enterprise surface                           | Same review depth, zero subscription, zero app install, zero metering. Loses hosted gating by design.                                       |
| Greptile                  | 30 dollars per dev per month with 50 reviews, 1 dollar per extra, per-author credits | Whole-codebase graph context, careful actionable comments          | Graph-grade context via full-diff plus caller tracing, unlimited re-reviews at no extra cost, any model. Loses the managed graph by design. |
| Pullfrog                  | SaaS with free personal and OSS tiers plus BYOK billing, GitHub App                  | Configurable automations beyond review (fix CI, conflicts, triage) | Review craft without accounts, keys-out, or meters. Loses automations beyond review by design.                                              |
| Human-only and gh aliases | Salaries plus waiting                                                                | Judgment, taste, context                                           | Every PR gets a grounded first pass in minutes. Humans spend attention on the hard 10 percent.                                              |

Every competitor charges
rent on the review (seats, credits, keys, dashboards) for craft that runs fine without
any of it. Verdict wins the single transaction (fetch, review, post, stop) on setup
cost, blast radius, model freedom, and verifiability per line of machinery.

## Complements, not competitors

clawpatch (machine-checked fix-to-revalidate loops for whole-repo campaigns), pstack
(verification culture and multi-model endurance) shaped this skill's discipline and compose after the verdict
posts. None of them reviews a stranger's PR on demand for near-zero setup, so none of
them is the alternative a buyer evaluates.

## Lineage

Verdict's review core descends from Pullfrog's open-source action, credited in
[NOTICE](https://github.com/stainless-code/verdict/blob/main/NOTICE.md). The craft is
shared on purpose. The meters are Verdict's disagreement, and the table above states
it plainly.
