AI review works. It is also metered, gated behind app installs, and hard to audit.
Verdict keeps the review and drops the meter.

## The five pains

1. **Review latency.** Human review is the bottleneck. PRs wait days for a look, or
   merge unreviewed.
2. **Keys and data held by someone else.** Hosted reviewers typically need a GitHub
   App install, webhook access, or API keys in their vault, and most meter usage. Some
   bill every re-review as a new review.
3. **Unverifiable AI output.** Many review bots post prose with no evidence level, no
   anchor check, and no record of what was claimed. Authors learn to skim past them.
4. **Anchor rot and re-report spam.** Comments land on lines that moved. The same
   finding is raised again on every push. Threads get resolved without a fix.
5. **Approval theater.** An approve on a diff nobody examined, or a blocking review
   with nothing behind it. The verdict says nothing about what was checked. Copilot code
   review can now submit an approval that satisfies a required-approval rule, so the
   question is no longer whether a bot may approve, but what backs the approval.

## How Verdict answers them

- **Latency.** `fetch` pulls the bundle, and on GitHub it needs no clone. Any agent you
  already run does the review. `post` lands one native GitHub review. Setup is `git`,
  `gh`, and one static `verdict` binary.
- **Trust.** Verdict has no server and sends nothing itself. Keys stay on your machine.
  The network calls are the `gh` reads and the one post you ask for. Diffs go only where
  your chosen model runs, and a local model keeps them on the machine. The source is open
  Go with no Go dependencies.
- **Verifiable output.** Every finding carries an evidence rung from `said` to
  `reproduced`. Unproven claims ship labeled `unproven` or are dropped. Anchors are
  checked against the diff hunks. Suggestions render as real GitHub suggestion blocks, or
  the post refuses to fake them.
- **Stable memory.** Findings carry a signature with no line numbers, a status, and an
  append-only history. Re-reviews do not report a recorded finding again, and threads
  retire by rule.
- **Verdicts with content.** A severity ladder maps to the author's next action. Binding
  verdicts need `--bind`, and approval is refused while findings are open. `calibrate`
  compares predictions with what happened to the PR.

## What it does not do

Verdict is not a hosted merge gate or check-run publisher, a daemon or watcher, a model
provider or proxy, or a dashboard or account system. `report --check` returns an exit
code that a CI job can honor. Verdict never posts a check run and never merges for you.
These limits keep setup to one binary and the blast radius to one post, and they keep
the skill runnable on any model.

## Where it concedes

Hosted reviewers keep the jobs that need a server: merge gating at enterprise scale,
fleet-wide policy, and hosted audit trails. Verdict does not do those. The review
itself runs on your machine with the model you choose.

Self-hosted means two different things here. Sourcery's Enterprise self-hosting is
their software in your infrastructure behind your SSO, and Pullfrog runs on your runner
under their control plane. Verdict has no server, no container and no account: one binary in
your shell.
