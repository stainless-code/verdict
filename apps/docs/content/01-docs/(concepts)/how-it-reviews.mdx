Three things separate a Verdict review from confident prose. Every claim carries
its proof. The machinery keeps good findings when it cannot tell. Memory
survives re-reviews.

## Evidence rungs

Every finding carries one rung: `said`, `pointed`, `walked`, `ran`, or
`reproduced`. The rung is the proof behind the claim, and it decides how far the
finding travels. A finding that was only `said` ships labeled `unproven` or is
dropped in triage. Nothing posts as fact that was never shown.

## Severity that decides

Severity is `critical`, `important`, or `informational`. It is stored when the
finding is written and never parsed from prose. It maps to the author's next
action, and machine rules gate the strong moves:

- Approving or requesting changes needs `--bind`. Without it the review posts
  as a comment.
- `APPROVE` is refused while findings are open or deferred, unless you pass
  `--force`.
- Binding `APPROVE` rechecks live review threads first. Unresolved threads the
  local store does not know about refuse the approval.

## Stated confidence

Confidence runs from 1 to 5. It is the agent's answer to "would I merge on this
review", informed by evidence depth, coverage, and rule support. The scale
never changes, so past verdicts stay comparable. `report --output` computes its
`Basis` line from the store: rigor rung counts, distinct cited rules, and
addressed findings counted as verified fixes. A posted body's `Basis` is written
by the agent and checked against the review's own comments.

## Keep-biased machinery

When the tool cannot prove an anchor wrong, it keeps the finding. Only an anchor
that is provably outside the diff moves to the review body, as a named note with
its reason. One bad anchor never fails a post.

Anchors are validated against the diff snapshot taken at fetch time, so a push
during the review does not move the target. A stale-SHA check and a double-post
guard run before the submit. `post` refuses a second completed post on the same
reviewed SHA unless you pass `--force`. A new commit re-arms it.

Suggestions render as real GitHub suggestion blocks, or the post refuses to fake
them. A review body past GitHub's 65,536-character limit splits: the first
review keeps the footer, and the rest post as follow-up comment reviews.

## Stable memory

Findings carry a signature with no line numbers in it, plus a status and an
append-only history. A rebase does not orphan them. Signatures are versioned
(`sig_version`, specified in the manual). They are the join key for deltas,
calibration, and outside policy tools.

Incremental re-reviews dedupe by signature, so a recorded finding is not
reported again unless the delta changes it. Threads retire by rule: a reformat
is not a fix, and human threads are left alone. Deferred work is explicit, never
silent.

Scoped rules compound the same way. `fetch` recalls matching rules into the
bundle as context, never as orders. When a finding that cites a rule flips to
`addressed`, the rule scores a hit. When it flips to `false-positive`, the rule
scores a false hit. After enough judgments, a rule with a high false-hit rate
retires to a tombstone. Nothing is deleted.

## Measured, not asserted

`calibrate` compares predictions with what happened on the PR: whether the cited
file changed, whether the thread was resolved, and the PR's merged state. It
reads live threads through your host's CLI and joins them against your local
store. Accuracy is a number from your own history, not a claim on a landing
page. Nothing is sent to Verdict or anyone else.
